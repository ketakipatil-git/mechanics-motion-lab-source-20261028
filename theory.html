FOUNDATIONS / NEWTON’S LAWS

Laws of motion

Newton’s laws describe how forces affect the motion of bodies. They are used throughout applied mechanics to analyze equilibrium and predict how moving systems respond to forces.

01

Newton’s first law · inertia

Statement — An object remains at rest or continues moving with constant velocity in a straight line unless acted on by a net external force.

Engineering application · statics — For a structure or machine component in static equilibrium, the vector sum of external forces and the sum of moments are zero:

ΣF = 0    and    ΣM = 0
02

Newton’s second law · acceleration and momentum

Statement — The time rate of change of a body’s linear momentum is equal to the net external force and is in the direction of that force.

Fnet = dp/dt    |    constant mass: Fnet = ma

Where Fnet is the net force, p is linear momentum, m is mass, and a is linear acceleration.

Engineering application · dynamics — Used to calculate accelerations, dynamic loads, motor and engine requirements, and the response of moving machinery.

03

Newton’s third law · action and reaction

Statement — When body A exerts a force on body B, body B simultaneously exerts a force of equal magnitude and opposite direction on body A. The two forces act on different bodies.

Engineering application — Supports free-body-diagram analysis, including reactions at pins, rollers, and fixed supports, and forces transferred between connected parts.

TOOLKIT / CONSTANT ACCELERATION

Equations of motion

For motion along a straight line with constant acceleration a, these equations connect initial velocity u, final velocity v, displacement s, and elapsed time t.

01

Velocity–time

v = u + at

Useful when displacement s is not known or not needed.

02

Displacement–time

s = ut + ½at²

Useful when final velocity v is not known or not needed.

03

Velocity–displacement

v² = u² + 2as

Useful when elapsed time t is not known or not needed.

Symbolsu = initial velocity · v = final velocity · a = constant acceleration · s = displacement · t = elapsed time. Choose a positive direction first and keep the signs of velocity, acceleration, and displacement consistent.
01 / LEVEL GROUND

Projectile motion on level ground

A projectile is an object moving after its launch while gravity is the only force considered. We ignore air resistance and use a flat reference plane. Horizontal and vertical motions can be analyzed independently because their acceleration components are independent.

Newton’s second law

ΣF = ma

With no horizontal force and weight acting downward, the ideal model has aₓ = 0 and aᵧ = −g, where g is gravitational acceleration.

Resolve the launch velocity

If the initial speed is u and its angle above the horizontal is θ, its components are:

uₓ = u cos θ
uᵧ = u sin θ

Position and velocity over time

x(t) = uₓt
y(t) = y₀ + uᵧt − ½gt²
vₓ(t) = uₓ
vᵧ(t) = uᵧ − gt

The trajectory is curved because horizontal position grows steadily while vertical position changes under constant downward acceleration. At the highest point, vᵧ = 0. The velocity vector is always tangent to the path.

Flight time, maximum height, and range

When the projectile lands at the same vertical level from which it was launched (y₀ = 0):

T = 2u sin θ / g
H = (u sin θ)² / 2g
R = u² sin(2θ) / g

For a nonzero launch height, landing occurs at y = 0; solve the vertical position equation and use its positive time solution. The horizontal range is then R = uₓT.

Speed and landing direction

|v| = √(vₓ² + vᵧ²)
φ = tan⁻¹(vᵧ / vₓ)

At landing, vᵧ is usually negative, so φ is below the horizontal. The simulator calculates the direction with both velocity components.

02 / INCLINED PLANE

Projectile motion above an incline

For a plane rising at angle α above the horizontal, define a local coordinate frame: s points uphill along the plane and h points perpendicular away from the plane. The launch angle β is measured above the plane. Gravity resolves into a component down the plane and a component into the surface.

aₛ = −g sin α
aₙ = −g cos α
uₛ = u cos β
uₙ = u sin β

Motion in plane-relative coordinates

s(t) = u cos β·t − ½g sin α·t²
h(t) = u sin β·t − ½g cos α·t²
vₛ(t) = u cos β − gt sin α
vₙ(t) = u sin β − gt cos α

Impact with the same straight incline occurs again when h(t) = 0, after the launch root t = 0. For an upward launch (β > 0):

T = 2u sin β / (g cos α)
Hnormal = u² sin² β / (2g cos α)
Rplane = s(T)

The simulator reports signed range along the plane, maximum perpendicular height, total final speed, and landing direction relative to the incline. The live readout also shows global horizontal/vertical velocity and acceleration, plus local along-plane/normal components.

Model assumptionsConstant gravity · no air resistance · straight, fixed incline · launch and impact on the same plane · point-particle motion.
